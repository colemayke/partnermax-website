01 / The founding
01 The founding story

Why PartnerMax
exists.

The Canadian telecom market is built around a few giants and a long tail of businesses too small for an enterprise team and too operationally serious for a retail account. We built the layer in between.

Rogers builds extraordinary commercial infrastructure — some of the most reliable wireless and fibre networks in the country. But the company is a national operator, and a national operator can’t personally serve every mid-sized business across Canada that needs its products and deserves a real conversation about them.

Our customers are the operators who don’t get returned calls from enterprise sales — and who keep the lights on for Canadian commerce anyway.

PartnerMax was founded to be that conversation. We hold a commercial channel license from Rogers, run the operations that turn a quote into an activated line, and stand behind a single monthly invoice. Our customers are the operators who don’t get returned calls from enterprise sales — the regional logistics firms, the multi-site manufacturers, the healthcare networks, the Crown corporations that still run on real telecom and don’t have a Bay Street procurement office to navigate it.

We don’t intermediate the technology. Rogers builds the network and the products; we license them, provision them, and stand behind the bill. The relationship is direct — just shaped for the businesses Rogers can’t personally cover.

02 / Mission
02 The mission

The role of channel
in Canadian telecom.

Channel partners aren’t middlemen — they’re the operational layer that lets carriers focus on building the network while businesses focus on running the business. Three principles guide how we play that role.

01 / The carrier’s job

Build the network.

Rogers does what only Rogers can do: own the spectrum, lay the fibre, operate the NOC, and meet the regulator. We don’t intermediate the technology — we sell what they build.

02 / The channel’s job

Translate it into a relationship.

A national carrier can’t know every Canadian business by name. We can. We size the deal, walk the floor, scope the rollout, and stay on the line when something needs sorting.

03 / The customer’s job

Run the business.

Our partners shouldn’t need to know the difference between a hosted PBX and a SIP trunk to get one quoted. We carry the complexity so they can keep their attention on their actual customers.

In our words

“A good channel partner is invisible when things are working, and the first person you call when they’re not. That’s the bar we hold ourselves to.” Dean Mayke · Managing Partner

04 How we work

Four commitments
we make in writing.

We codify these into every PartnerMax contract — they’re not values on a wall.

Commitment 01

Named contacts, not a queue.

Every partnership has a named channel manager and a named provisioning lead. You’ll have their direct line by the second meeting.

Commitment 02

One-day response SLA.

Every inbound from a partner gets a substantive reply within one business day — not a ticket number. Written into the contract.

Commitment 03

Quotes are quotes.

Written, itemized, valid for 30 days. The price you signed at activation is the price you see on the first invoice.

Commitment 04

No surprise renewals.

90-day renewal notice on every contract. No auto-rollover. You decide every cycle whether to keep the relationship.
